# Common Avatar Prompt

Use this unchanged for every avatar.

```text
Create an original, mature guardian-animal avatar for a white training card. Use a genuinely transparent background with no rectangle, scenery, halo, trail, mountain, mist, or colour field. Draw sparse but clearly recognisable vector-like line art: a decisive deep-slate primary silhouette, a few restrained pale-slate anatomical details, and one small quiet soft-gray personal mark on the shoulder or equivalent body area. The avatar must be immediately identifiable at a small card size.

The result should feel like premium outdoor field-guide line art: calm, adult, precise, and original. Use natural animal proportions and clear species-defining features. Keep the composition compact with generous transparent margin. Begin every avatar from a new, species-specific outer silhouette; do not reuse a generic head-and-neck base and merely swap ears, horns, or a muzzle.

No text, logo, watermark, card frame, background, filled silhouette, glow, gradient, lighting effect, realistic rendering, fur texture, hatching, brushwork, childish mascot proportions, exaggerated eyes, decorative geometry, armour, or copyrighted-character resemblance.
```

## Non-Negotiable Rules

- Use only slate linework, pale-slate secondary detail, one quiet soft-gray personal mark, and the planning-level symbol specified by the level prompt.
- Prefer one strong outer contour and a few meaningful internal lines over many faint or crossing paths.
- Species readability matters more than abstract symbolism.
- Each avatar must have its own head-to-neck proportion, ear or horn placement, muzzle shape, and pose. A different animal needs a genuinely different primary contour.
- The avatar represents only its own card. Never make its anatomy depend on card parents or children.
- The planning-level symbol may sit behind or beside the animal at restrained but clearly visible opacity. It identifies the planning level, not the individual card.
