# Mainstream Endurance Sources

## Scope

This file supports our interpretation in `philosophy.md`. It records institutional, academic, coaching-education, consensus, and peer-reviewed sources that inform the `mainstream_endurance` profile.

This profile deliberately excludes named coaching systems as primary sources. Do not use CTS, Evoke Endurance, Uphill Athlete, Some Work, All Play, Sharman Ultra, 80/20 Endurance, Lydiard, Daniels, Pfitzinger, or similar named-system material as the source backbone for this profile. Those sources may overlap with mainstream training principles, but their distinctive interpretations belong in their own profiles.

## Primary Textbooks And Academic Material

- American College of Sports Medicine. *ACSM's Guidelines for Exercise Testing and Prescription*, 12th edition. ACSM/Wolters Kluwer, 2026. Official ACSM book page: https://acsm.org/education-resources/books/guidelines-exercise-testing-prescription/
  - Supports the profile's use of FITT prescription logic, evidence-based exercise testing and prescription, and the idea that recommendations need to be adjusted across populations and applied settings.
  - Use for general prescription structure and terminology. Do not copy exact tables or clinical guidance into running cards without direct page-level review.

- Kenney, W. Larry; Wilmore, Jack H.; Costill, David L. *Physiology of Sport and Exercise*, 9th edition. Human Kinetics, 2025. Official publisher page: https://us.humankinetics.com/products/physiology-of-sport-and-exercise-8th-edition-with-hkpropel-access
  - Supports the academic exercise-physiology foundation: energy systems, cardiovascular and metabolic adaptation, fatigue, environmental physiology, and the body's responses to different exercise intensities.
  - Use as a physiology backbone, not as a source for exact running workouts unless reviewed at the relevant chapter/page level.

- National Strength and Conditioning Association. *Essentials of Strength Training and Conditioning*, 5th edition. NSCA/Human Kinetics. NSCA CSCS resource page: https://www.nsca.com/store/grouped-products/cscs-essential-collection/
  - Supports mainstream strength and conditioning concepts, training variables, programme design, and the integration of resistance training with sport preparation.
  - Use for broad strength-support reasoning. Review the current edition directly before making exact loading prescriptions.

- Bompa, Tudor O.; Buzzichelli, Carlo. *Periodization: Theory and Methodology of Training*, 6th edition. Human Kinetics, 2018. Official publisher page: https://www.human-kinetics.co.uk/9781492544807/periodization/
  - Supports the general planning vocabulary of training sessions, microcycles, macrocycles, annual plans, biomotor abilities, endurance methods, and peaking.
  - This is training-theory source material, not a named endurance-running system for this library. Use it for general periodisation concepts, not for treating one periodisation model as universal.

## Coaching Education And Consensus Material

- World Athletics. "WA Coaches Education and Certification System." Official page: https://worldathletics.org/development/education/coaches
  - Supports the claim that mainstream athletics coaching education is structured through levels, standardised materials, practical coaching skill, theoretical foundations, event-group specialisation, and planning across microcycles, mesocycles, macrocycles, annual plans, and multi-year development.
  - Use for the coaching-education framing, not exact endurance-session prescriptions.

- World Athletics. "Level II." Official page: https://worldathletics.org/organisation/our-people/coaches/level-ii
  - Supports the placement of principles of training, applied scientific concepts, periodisation, microcycle planning, and competition preparation inside mainstream athletics coaching education.

- USA Track & Field. "Education Standard." Official page: https://www.usatf.org/programs/coaches/coaches-registry/education-standard
  - Supports the idea that mainstream coaching credibility is tied to recognised education pathways, including USATF levels, cross country, marathon, World Athletics, NFHS, RRCA, and USTFCCCA routes.
  - Use as context for coaching education standards, not as a detailed source for training prescription.

## Research Reviews And Position Stands

- Garber, C. E., Blissmer, B., Deschenes, M. R., Franklin, B. A., Lamonte, M. J., Lee, I. M., Nieman, D. C., Swain, D. P., and American College of Sports Medicine. "American College of Sports Medicine position stand. Quantity and quality of exercise for developing and maintaining cardiorespiratory, musculoskeletal, and neuromotor fitness in apparently healthy adults: guidance for prescribing exercise." *Medicine & Science in Sports & Exercise*, 2011. PubMed: https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/21694556/
  - Supports the general principle that exercise prescription includes cardiorespiratory, resistance, flexibility, and neuromotor components, and that frequency, intensity, time, and type are core prescription variables.
  - This is health/fitness guidance for apparently healthy adults, not a competitive running plan. Use it for broad prescription structure only.

- Meeusen, R., Duclos, M., Foster, C., Fry, A., Gleeson, M., Nieman, D., Raglin, J., Rietjens, G., Steinacker, J., Urhausen, A., European College of Sport Science, and American College of Sports Medicine. "Prevention, diagnosis, and treatment of the overtraining syndrome: joint consensus statement." *Medicine & Science in Sports & Exercise*, 2013. PubMed: https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/23247672/
  - Supports the training-load boundary that overload must be balanced with recovery and that excessive overload plus inadequate recovery can lead to non-functional overreaching or overtraining syndrome.
  - Use cautiously: cards should not diagnose overtraining syndrome or replace medical evaluation.

- Soligard, T., Schwellnus, M., Alonso, J. M., Bahr, R., Clarsen, B., Dijkstra, H. P., Gabbett, T., Gleeson, M., Hagglund, M., Hutchinson, M. R., Janse van Rensburg, C., Khan, K. M., Meeusen, R., Orchard, J. W., Pluim, B. M., Raftery, M., Budgett, R., and Engebretsen, L. "How much is too much? Part 1: International Olympic Committee consensus statement on load in sport and risk of injury." *British Journal of Sports Medicine*, 2016. PubMed: https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/27535989/
  - Supports broad load-management reasoning: training and competition load, psychological load, travel, well-being, and injury risk all matter.
  - Use for cautious load framing, not for a single universal acute:chronic workload rule.

- Bourdon, P. C., Cardinale, M., Murray, A., Gastin, P., Kellmann, M., Varley, M. C., Gabbett, T. J., Coutts, A. J., Burgess, D. J., Gregson, W., and Cable, N. T. "Monitoring Athlete Training Loads: Consensus Statement." *International Journal of Sports Physiology and Performance*, 2017. PubMed: https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/28463642/
  - Supports the use of internal and external load monitoring as a decision aid, while preserving the need for interpretation.

- Seiler, S. "What is best practice for training intensity and duration distribution in endurance athletes?" *International Journal of Sports Physiology and Performance*, 2010. PubMed: https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/20861519/
  - Supports the mainstream observation that successful endurance training manipulates intensity, duration, and frequency, commonly using substantial low-intensity work plus carefully applied high-intensity work.
  - This overlaps with 80/20 Endurance but does not make this profile an 80/20 profile. Use only for broad intensity-distribution context.

- Esteve-Lanao, J., et al. "The training intensity distribution among well-trained and elite endurance athletes." *Frontiers in Physiology*, 2015. PMC: https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC4621419/
  - Supports the idea that intensity distribution is an important prescription variable and that polarized, pyramidal, and threshold-oriented distributions are debated in endurance research.
  - Use to preserve uncertainty around one best distribution.

- Bosquet, L., Montpetit, J., Arvisais, D., and Mujika, I. "Effects of tapering on performance: a meta-analysis." *Medicine & Science in Sports & Exercise*, 2007. PubMed: https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/17762369/
  - Supports tapering as a performance-relevant reduction in training load before competition.
  - Use for general taper logic. Do not apply one taper duration or reduction to every runner or race.

- Balsalobre-Fernandez, C., Santos-Concejero, J., and Grivas, G. V. "Effects of Strength Training on Running Economy in Highly Trained Runners: A Systematic Review With Meta-Analysis of Controlled Trials." *Journal of Strength and Conditioning Research*, 2016. PubMed: https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/26694507/
  - Supports the idea that strength training can improve running economy in trained middle- and long-distance runners.
  - Use for strength-support rationale, not for universal strength prescription.

- Alcazar-Ibanez, M., et al. "Effect of Strength Training Programs in Middle- and Long-Distance Runners' Economy at Different Running Speeds: A Systematic Review with Meta-analysis." *Sports Medicine*, 2024. PubMed: https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/38165636/
  - Supports more nuanced strength-training interpretation: effects depend on strength method, running speed, athlete level, and evidence certainty.
  - Use to avoid overclaiming that every kind of strength work has the same endurance benefit.

- Currier, B. S., D'Souza, A. C., Fiatarone Singh, M. A., Lowisz, C. V., Rawson, E. S., Schoenfeld, B. J., Smith-Ryan, A. E., Steen, J. P., Thomas, G. A., Triplett, N. T., Washington, T. A., Werner, T. J., and Phillips, S. M. "American College of Sports Medicine Position Stand. Resistance Training Prescription for Muscle Function, Hypertrophy, and Physical Performance in Healthy Adults: An Overview of Reviews." *Medicine & Science in Sports & Exercise*, 2026. PubMed: https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/41843416/
  - Supports current ACSM resistance-training prescription concepts for healthy adults.
  - Use for general resistance-training support and source updating; do not convert adult health prescriptions directly into trail-running performance sessions without review.

## Interpretation Notes

- This profile should state mainstream principles, not exact plans.
- A source supporting health-oriented exercise prescription does not automatically support competitive training prescriptions.
- A source supporting trained or elite athletes does not automatically apply to beginners.
- A source supporting road running does not automatically cover technical trail or mountain running.
- Intensity-distribution sources overlap with `80_20_endurance`; use them here only for broad mainstream context unless the card's distinctive logic belongs to 80/20.
- Periodisation sources overlap with `lydiard`; use them here only for general planning concepts unless the card's distinctive logic is Lydiard's base-first sequence and response-led timing.
- Strength and muscular-endurance sources overlap with `evoke_endurance`; use them here only for general strength-support reasoning unless the card uses Evoke's layered mountain-capacity model.
- Event specificity overlaps with `cts` and `sharman_ultra`; use this profile only where the reasoning remains general sport-science specificity rather than a named ultra-coaching method.
