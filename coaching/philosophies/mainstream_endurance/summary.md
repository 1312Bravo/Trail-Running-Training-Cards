# Mainstream Endurance Summary

## Status

**Available for card authoring after review.** This is our documented interpretation of mainstream evidence-informed endurance training: the broad exercise-science and coaching-education model commonly taught through university sport science, exercise physiology, strength and conditioning, and athletics-coaching pathways.

It is not a branded coaching system, a proprietary plan, or a claim that one universal programme fits every runner. It is the general training-method baseline that sits between the shared coaching foundation and the named philosophy profiles.

## In Brief

Mainstream endurance training organises running development around well-established training principles: specificity, overload, progression, individualisation, variation, recovery, and reversibility. It uses aerobic development as a central capacity, adds intensity deliberately, builds supporting strength and movement qualities where useful, and times more specific preparation toward the goal.

The profile is useful when a card reflects the general sport-science model taught in academic and coaching-education contexts, but is not materially shaped by CTS, Evoke Endurance, SWAP, Sharman Ultra, 80/20 Endurance, Lydiard, or another named system.

## Main Emphases

- Build consistent aerobic capacity through repeatable training before relying on specialised methods.
- Apply progressive overload carefully, using volume, frequency, duration, intensity, terrain, and strength load as variables.
- Keep recovery central because adaptation depends on the balance between training stress and restoration.
- Use intensity with a clear purpose, while avoiding chronic unplanned moderate-hard training.
- Increase specificity as the goal approaches, without abandoning general capacity or basic movement quality.
- Use periodisation, tapering, and monitoring as decision tools rather than rigid calendars.
- Include strength, mobility, coordination, and neuromotor work when they support running performance, durability, or health.
- Individualise prescriptions according to training history, readiness, goal demands, environment, and response.

## What It Does Not Mean

- It does not mean "average" training, generic plans, or lowest-common-denominator coaching.
- It does not mean copying one textbook progression without athlete context.
- It does not replace named philosophy profiles when their distinctive logic materially shapes a card.
- It does not justify exact numerical prescriptions unless the specific source and athlete context support them.
