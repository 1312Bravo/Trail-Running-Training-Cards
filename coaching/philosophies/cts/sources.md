# CTS Sources

## Scope

This source record supports our interpretation in `philosophy.md`. It does not reproduce CTS material or claim to be a complete bibliography.

Use the sources to revisit claims, add page-level notes from books, and distinguish direct CTS recommendations from our implementation choices. Most web material below was last reviewed on 2026-08-17.

## Primary Book

- **Jason Koop, _Training Essentials for Ultrarunning_, Second Edition.** CTS identifies this book as the central ultrarunning resource associated with its coaching method. This profile uses the book as a primary source category, but needs page-level notes before we make precise claims beyond the publicly available supporting material. [CTS book page](https://trainright.com/ultrarunning/)

## Official CTS Material

- **Jason Koop, "3 Steps for Creating Your Ultrarunning Long-Range Plan."** Supports the use of a season-level long-range plan to organise major themes such as intensity, volume, and nutrition practice, rather than treating it as a detailed daily schedule. Updated 2025-03-07. [Read the article](https://trainright.com/ultrarunning-long-range-plan-3-steps/)

- **Jason Koop, "The Hierarchy of Ultramarathon Training Needs."** Supports the priority given to consistent workload, rest, and a low-intensity-heavy annual distribution with strategic high-intensity work. It also frames ultramarathon performance as more than physical training alone. Updated 2025-03-07. [Read the article](https://trainright.com/hierarchy-ultramarathon-training-needs-jason-koop/)

- **Jason Koop, "CTS Key Running Workouts."** Supports the defined workout vocabulary and the distinction between recovery, endurance, steady, tempo, high-intensity interval, and stride work. It is a useful source for CTS terminology, not a universal prescription for every card. [Read the article](https://trainright.com/running-workouts/)

- **Jason Koop, "Which is Better: Block or Mixed-Intensity Periodization for Ultrarunners?"** Supports the interpretation that CTS often favours focused blocks for particular athletes and contexts, while recognising that periodisation is a coaching-design choice rather than one unavoidable rule. Updated 2025-03-07. [Read the article](https://trainright.com/block-mixed-intensity-periodization-ultrarunning/)

- **Jason Koop, "The New Ultramarathon Runner's Four Year Training Plan."** Supports the long-development view, gradual workload progression, learning, and the idea that advanced methods become more relevant after foundational experience and resilience. Updated 2025-03-07. [Read the article](https://trainright.com/new-ultramarathon-runner-four-year-training-plan/)

- **Jason Koop, "Training Density: What It Is and Why It Matters."** Supports the distinction between total volume and the arrangement or continuity of training, along with the need to adjust plans to real-life constraints. Updated 2025-03-07. [Read the article](https://trainright.com/training-density/)

- **Jason Koop, "Tracking Training for Trail and Ultramarathon Runners."** Supports prescribing trail training through time and perceived effort, and interpreting data in the actual terrain context. Updated 2025-03-07. [Read the article](https://trainright.com/tracking-training-trail-ultramarathon-runners/)

- **Jason Koop, "Ultramarathon Runners' Toolkit for Monitoring Training."** Supports the limitation of grade- or normalized-pace calculations on technical terrain and descents, including the additional coordination and musculoskeletal costs of downhill running. Updated 2025-03-07. [Read the article](https://trainright.com/ultramarathon-runner-toolkit-monitoring-training/)

- **Jason Koop, "Downhill Running: How to Go Faster and Hurt Less" and "Pre-Race Do's and Don'ts for Ultrarunners."** Support the importance of eccentric and neuromuscular downhill demands, plus the caution against treating one hard downhill session as the answer. Updated 2025-03-07. [Downhill article](https://trainright.com/downhill-running-training-go-faster-hurt-less/), [pre-race article](https://trainright.com/ultrarunners-pre-race-dos-donts/)

- **Jason Koop, "How to Implement Heavy Strength Training for Runners."** Supports a nuanced role for strength work: potential modest benefit, a preference for sufficiently demanding work when strength is used for performance, and the requirement that it not compromise the main running plan. Updated 2025-03-11. [Read the article](https://trainright.com/how-to-implement-heavy-strength-training-for-runners/)

- **Jason Koop, "The Role of Stress Relief in Training and Recovery."** Supports treating non-exercise stress and individual recovery strategies as part of training capacity, not as irrelevant background. Updated 2025-03-07. [Read the article](https://trainright.com/the-role-of-stress-relief-in-training-and-recovery/)

- **Jason Koop, "What Ultramarathon Runners Can Learn from IAAF Statement on Nutrition" and "Nutritional Challenges in Ultrarunning and How to Overcome Them."** Support rehearsing fueling and hydration under relevant conditions, starting with best practice, then individualising based on tolerance, preference, and race context. Updated 2025-03-07. [Nutrition practice article](https://trainright.com/iaaf-statement-nutrition-analysis-ultramarathon-runners/), [nutrition challenges article](https://trainright.com/nutritional-challenges-ultramarunning-overcome/)

## Interpretation Notes

- The philosophy file paraphrases and combines recurring ideas across sources. It should not be read as a quotation or exact CTS protocol.
- Numerical RPE ranges, durations, workload targets, and nutrition quantities in CTS source material remain within their original context. Do not transfer them into generic cards without a separate decision and source check.
- Add sources from the book, CTS podcast episodes, or newer official material here before expanding a CTS-specific claim.
