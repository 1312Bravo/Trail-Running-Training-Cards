# CTS Summary

## Status

**In development.** This is our summary of CTS ultrarunning coaching material, especially work associated with Jason Koop. It is not an official CTS document and does not yet make `cts` active for card authoring.

## In Brief

CTS is an event-demand-led ultrarunning system. It aims to build the capacity that matters most for the runner's target event through patient workload development, clear intensity definitions, long-range planning, and carefully chosen specific work.

The system does not reduce ultrarunning preparation to mileage, a single long run, or a collection of hard workouts. It treats fitness, recovery, terrain, descending, fueling, environmental preparation, and race execution as connected parts of one preparation problem.

## Main Emphases

- Start from the demands and likely limiters of the goal event rather than a fixed weekly template.
- Build durable, repeatable workload before relying on advanced methods or marginal gains.
- Use clearly defined intensity and strategically placed quality work inside a predominantly lower-intensity training year.
- Use a long-range plan to organise phases, trade-offs, recovery, and race-specific preparation.
- Interpret trail training through time, RPE, terrain, and total cost rather than pace alone.
- Rehearse relevant race-execution skills, including fueling, descending, equipment, and environmental strategy.

## What Makes It Distinct

The CTS interpretation is practical and cost-aware. A workout is useful only when it addresses a meaningful demand, fits the athlete's current capacity, and supports the larger plan rather than creating fatigue that undermines it.

It often uses focused blocks to develop a limiting quality, but treats them as a tool for the right athlete and moment, not as a permanent training identity. Data is useful when it improves a decision, but it does not replace coaching judgement or the runner's response.

## What It Does Not Mean

- It does not mean maximising mileage, vertical gain, or intensity for their own sake.
- It does not mean copying exact CTS workout numbers into a different runner's plan without context.
- It does not mean every trail session requires exact race-terrain replication.
- It does not mean a generic interval, long run, hill session, or strength workout automatically belongs to the `cts` profile.
