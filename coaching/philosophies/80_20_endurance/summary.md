# 80/20 Endurance Summary

## Status

**Available for card authoring.** This is our documented interpretation of 80/20 Endurance material associated with Matt Fitzgerald. It is not official 80/20 material; source limits still apply to exact prescriptions.

## In Brief

80/20 is a structured intensity-distribution approach: most training is deliberately low intensity, while moderate and high intensity are used purposefully, with clear workout structure and adequate recovery. Its deeper contribution is not a ratio alone but the hard/easy rhythm, individualised zones, reviewable execution, and course-specific adaptation that make the distribution useful.

## Main Emphases

- Keep the overall training pattern predominantly low intensity.
- Protect easy work from accidental moderate effort.
- Use moderate or high intensity only when it has a stated purpose and recovery space.
- Individualise zones and select the metric that remains useful on the actual terrain.
- Progress load through manageable cycles and adapt the calendar to durability.
- Treat load data as context rather than proof of specificity or readiness.

## What It Does Not Mean

- It does not require every day or week to meet one exact arithmetic ratio.
- It does not forbid controlled moderate or race-specific work.
- It does not let watch zones overrule terrain, conditions, athlete response, or mechanical cost.
