# 80/20 Endurance Coaching Philosophy

## Scope And Status

This file is our documented interpretation of published 80/20 Endurance running material. It is not an official 80/20 Endurance document. The profile is **in development** and should not label cards until we review the relevant primary books and plans.

## Philosophy In Brief

80/20 Endurance centres on intensity discipline: most work remains low intensity, while planned moderate and high-intensity work has a distinct purpose and target. Its system is structured enough to make execution repeatable, but it also instructs runners to use feel and judgement when exact targets no longer fit the situation.

## Coaching Principles

### Make Intensity Distribution Deliberate

The defining principle is that endurance training should be predominantly low intensity, with a smaller planned share of work at higher intensities. The purpose is not to create a moral rule about easy running; it is to prevent the common pattern in which athletes run too hard on easy days and too inconsistently on genuinely focused days.

The ratio is a framework, not a demand to force every session into an arithmetic category. The coach still needs to account for the athlete's history, event, training phase, and response. What matters is whether the overall pattern preserves enough low-intensity work to support training volume and enough focused work to develop the quality the goal requires.

### Define Workouts Precisely

80/20 material describes each workout through duration or distance, intensity, and structure. This makes the intended work visible and reviewable. A runner should know whether a session is an endurance run, a threshold-adjacent effort, a faster interval workout, or a lower-cost mixed stimulus.

Precision is useful only when it clarifies the intended stimulus. It should not turn a plan into a test of obedience, especially when the athlete faces terrain, heat, fatigue, or data limitations that make an exact target misleading.

### Personalise The Intensity Metric

The system allows pace, heart rate, power, and perceived effort to guide intensity, with zones set from an individual assessment. This recognises that different runners and different sessions may need different tools. The best metric is the one that helps the athlete execute the intended effort reliably in the real conditions.

No metric is treated as universal. Heart rate can lag at high intensity, pace can be distorted by terrain, and feel can be altered by fatigue or environment. The coach selects and interprets the metric rather than assuming that a watch zone is the training purpose itself.

### Avoid The Unplanned Moderate Rut

80/20 explicitly identifies an in-between intensity range that is often avoided in ordinary training because athletes can drift there without intending to. The system does allow controlled steady or race-specific work in that range when it has a purpose, but it resists making it the default pace for every run.

This gives the runner a useful decision rule: moderate work is not forbidden, but it should be chosen. If it is not the planned focus, easier work should remain easy enough to serve recovery and aerobic volume.

## Trail And Mountain Application

### Add Specificity Without Losing The Intensity Logic

80/20 material recognises that a hilly, trail, hot, or altitude event benefits from relevant exposure. The profile should therefore adapt terrain and route choice to the goal while preserving the intensity distribution that structures the system.

Trail specificity should not force a runner to chase a road pace on hills or make every session technical. The card should identify whether the important feature is intensity, climbing, surface, heat, duration, or skill, then use the terrain that makes that feature practical.

## Implications For Cards

### State The Intended Intensity Clearly

An 80/20-labelled card should identify the targeted intensity role and how it fits into the larger distribution. It should not leave a runner guessing whether an effort is meant to be easy, steady, threshold-adjacent, or high intensity.

### Allow Metric And Terrain Adaptation

The card should offer pace, heart-rate, power, or RPE guidance only as appropriate to the terrain and athlete. It should state when an alternative metric or reduced target is the correct way to preserve the intended session.
