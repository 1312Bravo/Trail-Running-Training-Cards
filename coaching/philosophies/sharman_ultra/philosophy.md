# Sharman Ultra Coaching Philosophy

## Scope And Status

This file is our documented interpretation of Sharman Ultra Coaching's published coaching description and coach information. It is not an official Sharman Ultra document and does not reproduce its plans, future book, articles, or coaching communication. The profile is available for card authoring, while its public source base still defines the team's coaching stance more clearly than its exact workout model.

Sharman Ultra describes a shared methodology adapted to each athlete, drawing on established endurance sources and practical ultrarunning experience. This profile treats that as an integrative coaching system. It does not attribute every detail of Lydiard, Noakes, Pfitzinger, NASM, or USATF to Sharman Ultra merely because the team names them as influences.

## Philosophy In Brief

Sharman Ultra treats coaching as more than sending a schedule. The method begins with knowing the runner and their real life, then adapts established endurance principles to an ultrarunning objective, teaches the runner to understand their response, and uses practical experience to make recommendations workable in the conditions of the event. Success is defined in athlete-specific terms, not only by a finishing time or placing.

Its distinctive contribution is therefore an adaptive, education-led interpretation of broad endurance knowledge for the varied realities of ultrarunning. The system should be used in this library where that individualisation and execution reasoning is visible, not as a generic label for any long run or trail race.

## Coaching Principles

### Know The Athlete Before Designing The Work

Sharman Ultra explicitly identifies rapport and understanding the athlete as a key principle. The coach should begin with goals, current fitness, history, lifestyle, available time, constraints, experience, and the athlete's own definition of success. This prevents a plan from treating an event name or distance as sufficient information about what the runner needs.

Individualisation remains accountable to a training rationale. It is not simply giving the runner preferred sessions. The coach asks which version of an established principle is most likely to be completed, understood, and absorbed in this athlete's circumstances. A smaller but repeatable dose can be more valuable than an idealised plan that clashes with the runner's life.

This profile should make the information-gathering step visible in cards. A Sharman Ultra-labelled card should not presume that "ultra runner" is enough context. It should point to the athlete factor that changes the prescription: time available, terrain access, current durability, prior ultra experience, confidence, recovery capacity, or the runner's specific definition of success.

### Maximise The Benefit Of Each Session

The team's published coaching material describes fitting a plan around the athlete's lifestyle to maximise the benefit of each training session. This does not mean every session must be hard or novel. It means each one should have a reason, an appropriate dose, and a place in the larger preparation. Unnecessary volume, symbolic suffering, and generic complexity are not automatically evidence of serious ultrarunning training.

For cards, this encourages a practical question: what capability does this work develop, and why is it the best use of the athlete's available recovery and time right now? A session should state its main purpose and not bury it under multiple competing goals.

The card should also make clear what can be removed. If the athlete has limited time or recovery, the best version of a session may preserve one key learning goal while dropping secondary terrain, duration, or intensity demands. That selectivity is part of maximising benefit, not a watered-down version of the plan.

### Adapt Established Endurance Knowledge For Ultras

Sharman Ultra names influences including Tim Noakes, Arthur Lydiard, Pete Pfitzinger, NASM, and USATF, alongside its own ultrarunning experience. That supports a broad, integrative approach rather than a single proprietary intensity system. Different goals may require different emphasis on aerobic development, durability, strength, speed, pacing, hiking, altitude, terrain, or event execution.

The boundary is important. A card cannot claim a precise Sharman Ultra method merely because it resembles a Lydiard base run or a Pfitzinger workout. The `sharman_ultra` label should mean that the integrative, ultra-specific reasoning and athlete adaptation are genuinely visible in the card.

### Teach The Runner To Read Their Body

Helping athletes understand their body and adapt training and racing more effectively is a stated Sharman Ultra benefit. The plan should therefore educate rather than demand blind compliance. The runner should know what a session develops, what a normal response looks like, which adjustment preserves the purpose, and when a response suggests the session should be simplified or moved.

Athlete education is not the same as asking a runner to self-diagnose every problem. It means the coach provides clear boundaries and useful decision cues. The athlete gains agency within a framework, while more serious or persistent concerns remain within appropriate professional scope.

Because the public source base does not yet define exact proprietary protocols, this educational layer is one of the safest ways to use the profile. A card can be specific about what the runner should notice and how that observation changes the next step without inventing a branded workout progression.

### Use Experience To Make Advice Practical

Sharman Ultra's coaches bring extensive racing and coaching experience across different endurance formats and locations. That can help identify logistical, terrain, pacing, and race-execution details that a generic training model may omit. Experience is valuable when it turns an abstract principle into a practical choice for a real course and runner.

Experience must still be interpreted honestly. A successful race strategy for an experienced coach is not universal proof. The profile distinguishes between broadly transferable endurance principles, course-specific guidance, and personal preference. The card should show that distinction rather than presenting an anecdote as a rule.

### Define Success With The Athlete

The team's published description frames success in athlete-specific terms. An ambitious time goal can be appropriate, but so can finishing with confidence, learning a new race format, returning after interruption, or balancing running with a full life. The definition of success changes how risk, progression, race strategy, and recovery should be judged.

This does not lower standards by default. It helps the coach choose standards that match the athlete's actual objective and current readiness. A card should offer purposeful training guidance without assuming every runner is optimising the same performance outcome.

### Keep The Method Open To Learning

Sharman Ultra describes its coaches as continuously learning, challenging conventional wisdom, and open to new findings. This supports evidence-aware coaching without rigid allegiance to a single system. When better information or athlete response contradicts an assumption, the plan can evolve.

The library should apply this through source traceability and clear uncertainty. A profile is a documented interpretation at a point in time, not a permanent claim that every named influence or current practice is settled fact.

## Training Load And Adaptation

### Fit Stress To The Life That Must Absorb It

An athlete-specific plan must account for the total context in which training is absorbed. Work schedule, family demands, travel, sleep, past injury, available terrain, experience, and emotional bandwidth can change the effective dose of a nominally identical session. Sharman Ultra's lifestyle-fit language supports treating these as genuine programming inputs, not excuses added after the plan fails.

The coach should protect the session's primary purpose while adapting its format, placement, duration, terrain, or frequency. If the right work cannot fit without creating unsustainable stress, a smaller or simpler version may be more faithful to the philosophy than forcing the original plan.

### Use A Plan As Guidance, Not A Test Of Obedience

The profile treats the plan as a tool for coaching, adjustment, and learning. A schedule supplies structure, but the athlete's response determines whether the next dose should be retained, progressed, or reduced. Good adherence means engaging with the training logic and communicating response, not mechanically completing a session that has become inappropriate.

Cards should be explicit about the non-negotiable stimulus and the variables that can be changed. This gives athletes useful agency while keeping the plan coherent.

### Protect Long-Term Participation

The team presents its method as helping runners take on bigger challenges while reducing the chance of injury in training and racing. No plan can eliminate risk, and the library should not promise that it can. The relevant coaching stance is to avoid needless risk: progress exposure sensibly, do not make every week a test, and respond early when the pattern suggests the athlete is not absorbing the work.

This is particularly relevant in ultrarunning because long duration can hide accumulating fatigue. A runner may complete a session successfully while setting up a poor response several days later. The coach needs to judge the whole pattern, not the completion status alone.

## Trail And Mountain Application

### Start With The Actual Demands Of The Event

Ultrarunning goals vary radically in elevation, technicality, altitude, temperature, surface, aid access, time on feet, and race format. Sharman Ultra's practical experience supports beginning with these actual demands rather than applying a distance-based template. A flat hundred-miler, a mountainous fifty-kilometre race, and a multiday event may all be called ultras but require different preparation.

The card should identify the relevant performance problem: sustained aerobic duration, climbing, hiking, technical descent, heat, altitude, fueling, pacing, night movement, or a combination. This lets the coach include course-specific work in proportion to its importance rather than adding every possible specialty.

### Make Specificity Accessible And Honest

Because plans are adapted to life and location, not every athlete will have direct access to mountains, heat, altitude, or technical terrain. The profile supports practical substitutions when they preserve a main stimulus: a treadmill, stairs, strength work, time-based prescription, flatter route, or controlled hike may all be useful.

The substitution must be honest. A stair session may reproduce climbing movement but not technical footing or outdoor descent. A treadmill can control gradient but not teach loose-surface coordination. The card should say what is preserved, what is missing, and whether direct exposure remains important before the goal.

### Teach Race Execution Alongside Fitness

Ultrarunning performance includes decisions made while tired: pacing, fueling, hydration, gear, hiking, problem-solving, and responding to changing conditions. Sharman Ultra's experience-led approach gives these practical skills a legitimate place in preparation. The athlete should not arrive with fitness but no understanding of how to use it over the event.

Execution practice should be staged. A beginner may first need a simple fueling routine in an aerobic long run; a more experienced runner may need a selective rehearsal of terrain, gear, duration, and pacing. The card should avoid turning every long run into a race simulation, since full rehearsal can create a recovery cost that exceeds its benefit.

### Respect Terrain's Hidden Recovery Cost

Technical terrain, long descents, altitude, heat, and long hiking can change the cost of a session without looking dramatic in conventional pace data. The profile should therefore consider muscle damage, coordination, energy use, and mental demand as well as distance and heart rate.

This leads to more practical scheduling. A technically demanding long run may need more recovery before the next quality session; a low-impact hike may provide useful endurance time with different mechanical cost. The coach should describe why the terrain is used and how it changes the recovery expectation.

## Knowledge And Decision-Making

### Combine Evidence, Expertise, And Athlete Feedback

An integrative method needs a transparent hierarchy of reasoning. Established endurance knowledge gives a starting point. Practical coaching and race experience help interpret it for a real goal. The athlete's response tests whether the chosen dose works in their life. None of these sources is sufficient alone.

Cards should reflect that balance. They should make evidence-grounded general guidance clear, treat course-specific practice as context-sensitive, and invite adjustment based on observable response rather than pretending that a generic schedule resolves every uncertainty.

### Keep Claims Proportionate To The Source Base

The public Sharman Ultra material supports a rich coaching stance, but not yet a complete published prescription for every session type. This profile must therefore stay more careful than CTS or Evoke when describing exact methods. It can be detailed about decision-making, individualisation, education, and execution without inventing a named proprietary progression.

That limitation is a strength. It keeps future cards honest and creates a clear research task: gather and review direct primary material before the profile is used for detailed Sharman-specific sessions.

## Implications For Cards

### Explain The Adjustment Logic

A Sharman Ultra-labelled card should state how duration, terrain, intensity, placement, or format can adapt while preserving the session's purpose. It should identify the meaningful constraint rather than presenting one exact schedule as the only acceptable version.

### Teach Rather Than Only Direct

The card should explain why the work belongs in the plan, what response to observe, and how that observation informs the next decision. That educational clarity is what makes the profile more than a generic list of alternatives.

### Connect Fitness To Race Execution

Where relevant, cards should show how the physical work connects to pacing, fueling, terrain judgement, gear, or other ultrarunning execution skills. The link must be proportionate to the card's role; not every session needs to rehearse every race detail.

The card should identify the decision being practised. "Race execution" is too broad unless it is tied to a concrete question, such as when to hike, how fueling timing holds on climbs, how gear changes pacing, or how the athlete responds to a long descent. This keeps the profile practical while respecting the limits of the current source base.

### Reserve The Profile Label For Real Structural Influence

Use `sharman_ultra` only where athlete-specific adaptation, educational guidance, practical ultra-execution reasoning, and integration of broad endurance principles materially shape the card. A generic long run with optional substitutions should not carry the Sharman Ultra label unless that distinctive logic is visible.

## Current Limits And Review Questions

- Review *The Art of Ultrarunning* when available, plus specific official articles, talks, and podcasts, before defining exact Sharman Ultra workouts or progression rules.
- Gather direct material on the team's approaches to intensity, long-run structure, hiking, altitude, and terrain before profile-labelling specialised cards.
- Keep the distinction between named influences and Sharman Ultra's own documented approach explicit.
