# Sharman Ultra Coaching Philosophy

## Scope And Status

This file is our documented interpretation of Sharman Ultra's published coaching description. It is not an official Sharman Ultra document. The profile is **in development** because the public source base defines its coaching stance more clearly than its exact training protocol.

## Philosophy In Brief

Sharman Ultra is an adaptive ultrarunning system that combines established endurance methods with practical experience and close attention to the athlete's life, goals, and response. It treats coaching as more than delivering a schedule: the guidance, adjustment, and athlete education are part of the method.

## Coaching Principles

### Start With The Individual Athlete

Sharman Ultra explicitly rejects a cookie-cutter approach and emphasises knowing the runner well. The plan should account for the athlete's goal, current fitness, history, lifestyle, available time, and practical constraints before it applies a training method.

Individualisation remains accountable to an underlying rationale. The coach is not simply matching sessions to preference; they are deciding which version of an established principle can deliver useful training with the greatest chance of being completed, understood, and absorbed.

### Adapt Established Endurance Knowledge For Ultras

Sharman Ultra describes its methodology as drawing from Tim Noakes, Arthur Lydiard, Pete Pfitzinger, NASM, USATF, and ultrarunning experience. The profile therefore treats it as an integrative system rather than an isolated doctrine. Its distinctive work is translating established endurance ideas to the varied demands of ultrarunning.

This has an important boundary: a card should not claim a precise Sharman Ultra protocol merely because it resembles one of those influences. The `sharman_ultra` label requires that the card's adaptive, ultra-specific, athlete-education logic is actually visible.

### Teach The Runner To Read Their Response

Published Sharman Ultra material identifies helping athletes understand their body and adapt training and racing more effectively as a core benefit of coaching. The plan should therefore educate the runner about purpose, response, and appropriate adjustment instead of asking for blind compliance.

This creates athlete agency without asking the athlete to self-diagnose every problem. The card should explain what the session is meant to develop, what a sensible adjustment preserves, and when the response suggests the runner should simplify, postpone, or seek more specific guidance.

### Use Experience Without Becoming Anecdotal

Sharman Ultra's ultrarunning experience is a meaningful source of course awareness, logistics, pacing, and practical race judgement. Experience is valuable when it helps the coach identify real demands and avoid naive recommendations that look sound on paper but fail in long events.

It must still be interpreted honestly. Experience is not proof that one approach fits all runners or all courses. The coach should distinguish between a broadly transferable principle, a course-specific lesson, and a personal preference derived from past racing.

### Keep The Method Open To Learning

Sharman Ultra describes its coaches as continuously learning, challenging conventional wisdom, and remaining open to new findings. This supports a philosophy that values evidence and reflection without becoming attached to a fixed plan because it has worked before.

For the library, this means sources and card claims should remain reviewable. When new evidence or practical experience changes the rationale, the profile should be updated rather than quietly retaining an outdated recommendation.

## Trail And Mountain Application

### Use Course Knowledge In Proportion To The Goal

The system's wide practical ultrarunning background supports goal-specific preparation. Terrain, altitude, heat, stage-race format, aid access, and technicality can all influence what should be practised. The coach should use relevant course knowledge to make a plan more useful, not to overload a runner with details that do not materially affect their event.

### Make Adaptation Practical

Because the system emphasises fitting training to real life, trail specificity should include access-aware alternatives. A runner may need a different route, treadmill, hill, strength exercise, or time-based prescription, provided the card clearly explains the intended stimulus and any direct mountain demand that remains unpractised.

## Implications For Cards

### Explain The Adjustment Logic

A Sharman-labelled card should state how the runner can adapt duration, terrain, effort, or placement while preserving the session's purpose. It should identify the meaningful constraint rather than present an exact schedule as the only acceptable execution.

### Teach Rather Than Only Direct

The card should help the runner understand why the session belongs in the plan and what response to observe. This is what makes the philosophy genuinely coach-led rather than a generic list of alternatives.
