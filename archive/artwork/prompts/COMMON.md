# Common Avatar Prompt

Use this unchanged for every avatar.

```text
Create an original, mature guardian-animal avatar for a white training card. Use a genuinely transparent background with no rectangle, scenery, halo, trail, mountain, mist, colour field, block emblem, or planning-level symbol. Draw a clear full-body semi-realistic SVG animal: a decisive deep-slate primary silhouette, restrained pale-slate anatomical detail, optional very pale interior fill for readability, and one small quiet soft-gray personal mark placed beside the animal, not on the centre of the body. The avatar must be immediately identifiable at a small card size.

The result should feel like premium outdoor field-guide line art: calm, adult, precise, and original. Use natural animal proportions and clear species-defining features. Prefer a readable full-body side, flying, perched, standing, leaping, or upright pose over a cropped bust or abstract looped body. Keep the composition compact with generous transparent margin. Begin every avatar from a new, species-specific outer silhouette; do not reuse a generic body base and merely swap ears, horns, beaks, or a muzzle.

No text, logo, watermark, card frame, background, glow, gradient, lighting effect, photorealistic rendering, heavy fur texture, dense hatching, brushwork, childish mascot proportions, exaggerated eyes, decorative geometry, armour, planning-level symbol, or copyrighted-character resemblance.
```

## Non-Negotiable Rules

- Use slate linework, pale-slate secondary detail, optional very pale fill, and one quiet soft-gray personal mark.
- Prefer one strong outer contour and a few meaningful internal lines over many faint or crossing paths.
- Species readability matters more than abstract symbolism.
- Each avatar must have its own body proportion, tail/wing/leg shape, ear or horn placement, muzzle/beak shape, and pose. A different animal needs a genuinely different primary contour.
- Do not bulk-generate the full set from one shared body grammar or template. Similar animals may share a visual family, but each species needs a distinct pose and silhouette.
- The avatar represents only its own card. Never make its anatomy depend on card parents or children.
- Planning-level symbols, colour, and scenery are rendered by the app around the avatar. They must not be baked into the avatar SVG.

## Realism Direction

- Use full-body silhouettes by default. Only crop to a bust if the full animal genuinely cannot read at card size.
- Make the species-defining feature unmistakable: eagle wings and hooked beak, ibex horns, chamois hooked horns, wolf running/howling posture, fox tail and muzzle, deer antlers, bird wing shape, etc.
- Avoid clever single-line abstractions if they make the animal hard to name.
- Add small filled planes only to clarify anatomy; the avatar should still feel like SVG field-guide art, not a bitmap illustration.

## Batch Workflow

- Test 4-6 representative animals first before replacing a block or full set.
- Include at least one bird, one horned mountain animal, one predator, and one small animal in the sample.
- Review samples side by side at card size before applying the direction broadly.
- Preserve approved existing avatars unless the replacement is visibly clearer.
